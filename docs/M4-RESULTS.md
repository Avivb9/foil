# M4 results — does adding the missing proof change the deal?

Full numbers: [AB-POSTHOG-PROOF.md](AB-POSTHOG-PROOF.md) (generated by `npx tsx scripts/ab-report.ts data/ab-posthog-proof-v1.json docs/AB-POSTHOG-PROOF.md`)

## The test

In the baseline, every committee ended in "no decision", and PostHog was blocked by two requirement owners: the technical evaluator (works with our Snowflake warehouse) and procurement (predictable pricing).

We added **two short paragraphs** to the frozen copy of PostHog's pricing page, one proving each point. The other five pages stayed byte-identical. Then we re-ran the same committee, with the same model and prompt, 18 times and compared against 20 baseline runs.

The added copy is hypothetical, written for this test; it is not PostHog's real page.

## Results

| | Before | After |
|---|---|---|
| Technical evaluator blocks PostHog on the warehouse | **80%** | **0%** (p < 0.001) |
| Committee members backing PostHog (of 5) | **1.0** | **3.1** |
| Procurement blocks PostHog on pricing | 70% | 94% (p = 0.05) |
| PostHog wins the deal | 0% | 6% |
| Mixpanel / Amplitude blocked (unedited, placebo) | 100% / 100% | 100% / 100% |

## What it means

1. **One paragraph of the right proof flipped a stakeholder.** The warehouse block went from 80% to zero, and support for PostHog tripled. That's the clearest possible signal that this was a messaging gap, not a product gap.
2. **The pricing paragraph backfired.** Procurement got *more* suspicious (70% to 94%, borderline significant). Our reading, not yet tested: "spending cap" and "stop billing" draw attention to usage-based billing instead of answering "what will this cost us?".
3. **The deal is still lost, on one objection.** With 3 of 5 members behind PostHog, the committee still ends in no decision because procurement owns pricing and isn't convinced. That's how real B2B deals stall.
4. **The experiment is clean.** Vendors that weren't edited didn't move, so the change comes from the edit, not from noise in the model.

## Next test

Rewrite only the pricing paragraph to answer procurement's actual question: show the price at this buyer's volume and a 12-month cost range. Keep the warehouse paragraph. Re-run and compare. This is exactly the loop a PMM would run before shipping a pricing page.
