# M3 results — is the AI judge any good?

Full numbers: [JUDGE-EVAL.md](JUDGE-EVAL.md) (generated by `npx tsx scripts/judge-report.ts` from [`data/judge-eval-v1.json`](../data/judge-eval-v1.json))

## What was tested

30 items: 3 vendors × 10 requirements (the deal's 4 plus 6 probes such as SOC 2, SSO and a free plan). Each item shows the exact page excerpts the AI sees. The judge says met / not met / unclear, scored against an answer key labelled by a PMM.

## Findings

1. **The judge is far better than keyword matching, though not perfect.** Gemini agrees with the PMM on 87% of items (κ 0.73, "substantial agreement"). The keyword baseline gets 77% but κ 0.37, because it says "met" whenever a related word appears.
2. **Every disagreement is about pricing or the warehouse requirement**, the same two that block every vendor in the baseline. On clear-cut requirements (EU hosting, SSO, free plan, session replay, experiments) the judge matches the PMM every time.
3. **The judge is pessimistic about pricing.** It marks Amplitude's usage-based pricing "not met" in 3 of 3 runs, where the PMM read the same excerpts as "met".

## What this means

- The M2 blocker "nobody proves predictable pricing" is partly the AI's harshness, and partly the requirement not being written as a testable statement. Next iteration: split it into *"pricing shows a usage cap or billing limit"* and *"the price at the buyer's volume is shown"*.
- Run-to-run stability is high (the same verdict in all 3 repeats on 90% of items), so the M2 differences between vendors are not noise.
- Groq's judge runs are scheduled (it hit its daily token cap); its column will appear when the report is re-run.
