# M3 results — is the AI judge any good?

Full numbers: [JUDGE-EVAL.md](JUDGE-EVAL.md) (generated by `npx tsx scripts/judge-report.ts` from [`data/judge-eval-v1.json`](../data/judge-eval-v1.json))

## What was tested

30 items: 3 vendors × 10 requirements (the deal's 4 plus 6 probes such as SOC 2, SSO and a free plan). Each item shows the exact page excerpts the AI sees. The judge says met / not met / unclear, and we compare it to reference labels.

**Reference labels** were made by Claude Opus 5.5 from the same excerpts, with a written reason for each item, not by a human PMM. That makes this a "cheap free model vs. a stronger model" check. A human spot-check (1 item so far) is kept separately. Disclosure: the labeller had seen Gemini's answers before labelling.

## Findings

1. **The judge is far better than keyword matching, though not perfect.** Gemini agrees on 87% of items (κ 0.74, "substantial agreement"). The keyword baseline gets 73% but κ 0.33, because it says "met" whenever a related word appears.
2. **Every disagreement is about pricing or the warehouse requirement**, the same two that block every vendor in the baseline. On clear-cut requirements (EU hosting, SSO, free plan, session replay, experiments) the judge matches the reference every time.
3. **The judge is pessimistic about pricing.** It marks usage-based pricing "not met" (3 of 3 repeats for Mixpanel and Amplitude) where the excerpts don't actually prove it's unpredictable.
4. **The human spot-check disagreed with the reference on a pricing item too.** "Predictable pricing" is ambiguous even for people.

## What this means

- The blocker finding from M2 ("nobody proves predictable pricing") is partly the requirement's fault: it isn't written as a testable statement. Fix for M4: split it into two checks, *"pricing shows a usage cap or billing limit"* and *"the price at the buyer's volume is shown"*, and re-run.
- Run-to-run stability is high (the same verdict in all 3 repeats on 90% of items), so the M2 differences between vendors are not noise.
- Groq's judge runs are scheduled (it hit its daily token cap); its column will appear when the report is re-run.
