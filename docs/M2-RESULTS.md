# M2 results — a five-person buying committee, decided by code

Date: 2026-11-19 · Snapshot: `product-analytics-eu-series-b@2026-11-19-b69092a140` · Prompt: `committee-v0.3` · Decision rule: `v2`
Full numbers: [BASELINE-REPORT.md](BASELINE-REPORT.md) (generated by `npm run report` from [`data/baseline-v1.json`](../data/baseline-v1.json))

## Headline findings

**1. For the PMM: nobody proves the two things this buyer cares most about.**
18 of 18 committees (9 Gemini, 9 Groq) end in *no decision*. Self-serve analytics is settled for all three vendors, and EU hosting is shown by PostHog and Amplitude. What blocks every vendor is the same two gaps:
- **"Works with our warehouse, no second source of truth"**: the technical evaluator could not confirm it for any vendor in most runs (56–100%).
- **"Predictable pricing"**: procurement flagged it for every vendor in most runs (56–100%).
- Mixpanel additionally never shows EU hosting on the pages read (unclear in 18 of 18 runs). Mixpanel may offer it, but its homepage and pricing page don't say so where a buyer would look.

That is a positioning readout, not a feature gap: the next test (M4) is to add warehouse and pricing proof to one vendor's page and measure whether the committee moves.

**2. For the engineer: the two model families are not equally trustworthy as judges.**

| | Gemini 3.5 Flash Lite | Groq gpt-oss-120b |
|---|---|---|
| Quotes that exist on the page | 99% | 89% |
| Agents contradicting their own verdicts | 2% | 38% |

Both differences are significant (p < 0.0001). Gemini stays the primary model; Groq is the cross-check.
Contradictions don't distort outcomes, because outcomes are computed from per-requirement verdicts, never from the model's stated preference. That design choice is now measurably paying off.

**3. The models agree on what's clear and split on what's ambiguous.**
They match on 7 of 12 vendor × requirement verdicts (58%). Every disagreement is the same shape: Gemini says "not met" where Groq says "unclear" (pricing, warehouse). Model temperament is a variable, so Foil reports both instead of averaging it away.

**4. The data caught a bug in my decision rule, and the fix cost zero model calls.**
Rule v1 ignored "no decision" votes, so run 19 counted as a PostHog *win* with 1 of 5 votes. Rule v2 treats "no decision" as a candidate. Because the outcome is computed by code from stored verdicts, all 18 runs were re-scored instantly with no new LLM calls.

## Built in M2

- **Evidence packs** (`api/_lib/evidence.ts`): coverage-first BM25 retrieval per role, ~2.8K chars per vendor; the same evidence for every model. Prompts went from ~15K to ~3–4K tokens.
- **Per-requirement verdicts with owners** (`api/_lib/agent.ts`, `api/_lib/demo-deal.ts`): a "met" without a verified quote is downgraded to "unclear"; for a requirement's owner, "unclear" blocks.
- **Committee by code** (`api/_lib/committee.ts`): owner blockers → eligibility → role votes → plurality including "no decision" → economic-buyer veto. `tallyDecision` is pure, so stored runs can be re-scored.
- **Stats** (`api/_lib/stats.ts`): Wilson intervals, two-proportion test, mode.
- **Unattended runner**: Supabase `pg_cron` calls the batch endpoint every 5 minutes in a quota-safe mode (agents one at a time, ≤2 tries), and stops when the target is reached. Frozen snapshot and runs live in an isolated `foil` schema behind secret-checked functions.
- **Report generator** (`scripts/report.ts`): deterministic; reproduces every number from the data file.
- 26 unit tests.

## Cost and operations

- $0. Gemini and Groq free tiers; Supabase and Vercel free plans.
- Gemini's free tier returned 503 "high demand" for hours; the scheduler absorbed it. Groq stopped at its 200K tokens/day limit after 9 runs.
- The scheduler keeps topping up to 20 Gemini and 10 Groq runs; re-export and re-run `npm run report` to update.

## Known limits of this baseline

- n = 9 per model: outcome CIs are wide (no-decision rate 70–100%). The reliability gaps are large enough to be significant anyway.
- One deal, three vendors, two pages each. The agents read text excerpts, not full sites, reviews or analyst reports.
- Free-tier models are not the strongest available.
